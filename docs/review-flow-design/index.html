Real reviews, asked for after the first lesson

Three parts, three designs each: how we ask a student for a review, the review form, and how reviews and "lessons taught" show on a tutor's profile. Every design is shown on a computer and on a 390 px phone; Light, Dark and System switch the colours. Pick one design for each part and I will build it.

The rules behind all nine designs. Only a student who had a lesson held can review. One review for each student and tutor. A tutor cannot edit or delete a review, only reply once. The student writes with a signed link (no account), and the tutor's profile counts only lessons that were held. Reviews that exist today stay as they are.

1 · Asking for the review after the first lesson

Most students will not write a review unless asked at the right moment, and asking too much feels like spam. The first lesson is the best moment: the memory is fresh and the student is deciding whether to continue.

My suggestion: A. A star in the email is the lowest friction (one tap), the page that opens already holds the rating, and it needs no new screen in the student page. C asks a better question but needs two steps. B depends on the student opening the portal.
A

An email with five stars to tap

Sent the morning after the first lesson. Tapping a star opens the form with that rating already chosen.

Good for

  • Lowest effort: one tap
  • No account and no portal visit needed
  • Works in every email app (the stars are links)

Watch out for

  • Some email apps preview links, so the link must only open the form and never save a rating by itself
  • A star tap can be a mis-tap, so the form shows it and lets the student change it
B

A banner in the student portal, plus one plain email

The student page shows "How was your first lesson?" with stars once the lesson is over. The email only says the lesson is finished and has one button.

Good for

  • Feels like part of the product, not marketing
  • The banner can stay until answered or dismissed

Watch out for

  • Students who book without an account may never open the portal
  • More to build: a banner in the portal and the email
C

One yes-or-no question, then the stars

"Would you book Tomás again?" is easier to answer than a rating. A "yes" leads to the stars and a few words, a "not sure" to private feedback for the tutor. One reminder, then we stop.

Good for

  • Answers are more honest than stars
  • "Not sure" gives the tutor private feedback instead of a public low rating, without hiding anything from the student

Watch out for

  • Two steps lose some students
  • Needs a place for private feedback, which is more to build

2 · The review form

What the student sees after tapping the link. It must be short, say plainly what becomes public, and make clear the review is real.

My suggestion: A. Stars first, a few tags, optional words, on one screen: it takes under a minute and the tags give search engines and future students short, comparable facts. B is gentler on a phone but longer. C collects more signal but three ratings feel like a survey.
A

One card: stars, tags, a few words

Stars are required, the tags and the text are optional. It says what is public, for how long it can be edited, and who may review.

Good for

  • Fastest: under a minute
  • Tags become short, comparable facts on the profile
  • One screen to build

Watch out for

  • Long text boxes on a phone are tiring, so the text is optional and capped at 500 characters
B

Three short steps

Stars, then "What went well?" with big answers, then optional words. A progress bar and Back on each step.

Good for

  • Easy to do one-handed on a phone
  • Each step can be explained

Watch out for

  • More taps; a step is lost when someone leaves
  • Three screens to build and test
C

Three ratings and words

Teaching, preparation and communication each get stars; the overall rating is their average.

Good for

  • More detail for the tutor to learn from
  • Harder to give a vague 5 stars

Watch out for

  • Feels like a survey and may lower the number of reviews
  • The average hides which part was weak unless the profile shows all three

3 · Reviews and lessons taught on the tutor's profile

A new tutor has no reviews, so the number of lessons taught on Tutafy is the second proof. Both must be easy to trust: every review says how many lessons that student took, and the lesson count only counts lessons that were held.

My suggestion: A. The classic summary (average, breakdown, count, lessons taught) is what students from Airbnb, Amazon and iTalki already read, so it needs no learning. C is the most persuasive once a tutor has many reviews, and could be added later. B is the lightest but hides the breakdown.
A

A summary card, then the reviews

The average with a breakdown by stars, the number of reviews and lessons taught, a line saying every review is from a student who took a lesson, then the reviews.

Good for

  • Familiar, trusted, easy to read
  • The breakdown shows that a 4.9 is not made of 5s alone

Watch out for

  • Takes a lot of height on a phone before the first review

What I need from you

1. Pick one design for each part

Part 1: A, B or C. Part 2: A, B or C. Part 3: A, B or C. Or say "A for the form, B for the profile".

2. Rules to confirm (they change what I build)

  • Low ratings: are shown like any other review (my suggestion, it is what makes the rest believable). The tutor can reply once, publicly, but cannot hide or edit it.
  • Edit window: the student can change their own review for 7 days.
  • Names: first name and last initial (Maria K.), never an email.
  • Existing reviews: kept as they are. They get no "lessons taken" line until a lesson can be matched to them.
  • Abuse: a review link works once and only for a held lesson; the same student cannot review the same tutor twice; Tutafy can remove a review only for abuse (hate, spam, a clearly different tutor), and says so on the page.

Notes on all nine designs

  • Words are English only. Targets are at least 44 px. Text is at least 4.5 to 1 against its background in both colour schemes. A star is never the only way to read a rating: the number is always written.
  • Photos are initials tiles, like the live directory. Nothing is sent or saved in these samples.
